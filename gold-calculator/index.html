    Help and guide

    What each option means, how the bill is worked out, and the rules for buying gold in India.

    How to use this calculator
    1. Pick By weight if you know how heavy the piece is, By budget to see how many grams your money buys, or Check bill to break down a total the shop gave you.
    2. Choose the gold type (karat) of the piece.
    3. Check the gold rate. Use today’s rate, or type the rate on your jeweller’s board.
    4. Enter the weight and the making charge the shop quotes.
    5. Open More charges for wastage, stones, hallmark fee and discounts, and Trade in old gold if you are exchanging old jewellery.
    6. Compare the bill with the shop’s bill line by line. Tap Share bill to send a link with the exact same numbers.

    The calculator remembers your gold type, the karat of your rate and whether you price by weight or by budget. Amounts and rates start fresh each time.

    How the gold bill is worked out

    The bill is built the same way Indian jewellers build theirs:

    1. Gold = gold weight × rate per gram for that karat. Gold weight is total weight minus stone weight.
    2. + Wastage = a percentage of the gold value, if the shop charges it.
    3. + Making charges, as a percentage of gold value, an amount per gram, or a fixed amount.
    4. − Discount on making, if the shop is offering one.
    5. + Stones and + Hallmark fee, if any.
    6. + GST at 3% on everything above.
    7. − Old gold you trade in, which comes off the final amount.

    In By budget mode the calculator runs this backwards and finds the most gold, to the nearest milligram, whose total fits your budget.

    Checking a jeweller’s bill

    Jewellers usually tell you the gold type, the gold rate, the weight and the final amount. Choose Check bill and enter those four. The calculator works backwards:

    Bill total − GST − gold value − wastage − stones − hallmark = making charges

    • GST is taken as 3% of the price before GST. If the bill shows the GST amount, enter it and the calculator uses that instead and tells you its real rate.
    • If the bill states the making charge, enter it. Any amount left over shows as Not explained; ask the shop what it is for.
    • Add wastage, stone weight, stone price or a hallmark fee under More charges if the bill lists them, so they aren’t counted as making.

    You then see the making charge as a percentage of the gold value and per gram, how it compares with what value-conscious buyers pay, the all-in price per gram, and how the shop’s rate compares with today’s.

    Gold type: 24K, 22K, 18K and others

    Karat tells you how much of the metal is pure gold. The number on the hallmark is the purity in parts per thousand.

    KaratHallmarkPure goldUsed for
    24K99999.9%Coins and bars
    22K91691.6%Most Indian jewellery
    20K83383.3%Some regional jewellery
    18K75075%Diamond and stone jewellery
    14K58558.5%Light and daily-wear pieces
    9K37537.5%Low-cost pieces

    Lower karat gold costs less per gram because it has less gold in it. The calculator works out each karat’s rate from the 24K rate using these purities.

    Gold rate: live, your own, and any karat

    Today’s rate is worked out from the world gold price, which loads automatically:

    World price per ounce ÷ 31.1035 g × (1 + import duty) + local premium = 24K rate per gram

    Import duty is 15% (10% customs duty plus 5% AIDC since May 2026). The local premium, ₹150 a gram by default, brings the result close to city board rates. You can change both under Where today’s rate comes from.

    Your own rate: type the rate from your jeweller’s board into the rate box. Pick the karat it is for first. If the board shows a 24K rate and you are buying 22K, choose 24K and type it in; the calculator works out the 22K rate for you.

    When you use your own rate, the calculator shows today’s live rate next to it, the difference, and what the bill would be at today’s rate. Tap Use today’s rate to switch back. A rate that came in a shared link shows the date it was shared.

    Shop board rates don’t include GST. GST is added to the bill separately. Rates differ a little between cities and shops, so your jeweller’s rate is always the one that counts.

    Weight: total weight and gold weight

    Enter the total weight printed on the tag or shown on the shop’s scale, in grams. Jewellers weigh to the milligram, for example 12.450 g.

    If the piece has stones, enter their weight in More charges. The calculator takes it out, so you only pay the gold rate on the gold. Stones are charged separately at their own price.

    Ask the shop to show the weight on the scale, and check it against the tag and the bill.

    Budget: how much gold can I buy?

    Choose By budget and enter the most you want to pay. The calculator finds the largest gold weight whose total, with making charges, other charges and GST, stays within your budget.

    If you are trading in old gold, its value is added to your budget, so you can buy more.

    Fixed charges such as a hallmark fee or fixed making charge are paid whatever the weight. A very small budget may not cover them.

    Making charges on gold jewellery

    Making charges pay for the craftsmanship. Shops charge them in one of three ways:

    • % of gold: a percentage of the gold value. Plain machine-made chains go for 6% to 10%, and some local shops start at about 5%. Plain 22K jewellery at a local shop is usually 8% to 15% and negotiable. Branded stores often charge 12% to 20%, and rings and detailed handmade work 14% to 25%.
    • ₹ per gram: a fixed amount for every gram, common for chains, bangles and light jewellery; often ₹300 to ₹900 a gram for chains.
    • Fixed ₹: one amount for the whole piece.

    How the calculator rates a making charge:

    % of goldWhat it means
    Up to 6%A very good deal, typical of plain machine-made pieces
    6% to 12%Fair for plain 22K jewellery at a local shop
    12% to 20%On the high side, like branded stores; ask for less
    Above 20%High; only for intricate, handmade or bridal pieces

    Making charges are often negotiable at local shops, and brands run festival offers. Enter any offer as Discount on making. A low making charge is only a good deal if the piece is hallmarked and the gold rate is fair, so check both.

    Wastage, stones, hallmark fee and GST

    Wastage (also called VA, value addition) covers gold lost while making the piece. It is common in South India, usually 6% to 14% of the gold value. Some shops include it in making charges and some don’t charge it at all, so ask.

    Stone price is the price of any stones, diamonds or beads, charged separately from the gold.

    Hallmark fee: every hallmarked piece carries the BIS logo, its purity and a 6-digit HUID code. Shops pay BIS ₹75 per piece for hallmarking (since September 2026). It is not a compulsory separate charge to you, so it is empty by default. Add it only if your shop lists it on the bill.

    GST on gold jewellery is 3% of the full price, making charges included. The 5% rate you may read about applies to job work between businesses, not to you.

    You can check any HUID code in the BIS Care app to confirm the purity and the jeweller.

    Trading in old gold

    Most jewellers let you exchange old gold for new jewellery. Its value comes off your bill:

    Old gold value = weight × rate per gram × (1 − melting loss)

    • Weight: weigh it without stones.
    • Gold type: the shop tests the purity. Old pieces without a hallmark may test lower than you expect.
    • Rate the shop gives you: filled from the gold rate you are using (yours or today’s) for that karat. Change it to what the shop offers.
    • Melting loss: some shops cut a few percent for loss when melting. Ask before you agree.

    If your old gold is worth more than the new piece, the result shows what the shop pays you.

    PAN card and cash limits for buying gold
    • For purchases above ₹2 lakh you must give your PAN, however you pay.
    • A shop can’t accept ₹2 lakh or more in cash for one purchase (Section 269ST of the Income Tax Act). Splitting the bill doesn’t get around this. Pay by UPI, card or bank transfer.
    • Ask for a proper bill showing the purity, gold weight, rate per gram, making charges, wastage, hallmark fee and GST as separate lines.
    • Check that the HUID on the bill matches the piece.

    Saving and sharing a gold bill

    This browser remembers only your gold type, the karat of your rate, and By weight or By budget. Weights, charges and your own rate are not saved. Nothing is sent anywhere. Clear all resets the calculator, and you can undo it for a few seconds.

    Share bill creates a link that rebuilds this exact bill, including the gold rate you used. Anyone who opens it sees the same numbers. Opening a shared link doesn’t change your own saved bill unless you edit the shared one.
